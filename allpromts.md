How these differ from the standard categories and why:

Jacket Kurti and Jacket Lehenga — both require layering logic that standard single-garment prompts don't have. The key instruction is that the outer layer must cast shadow on the inner layer. Without this, GPT merges them into one flat garment.

Dhoti Pant — the most technically unique. The dhoti drape between the legs is not a seam — it's a gathered fabric fold. GPT defaults to rendering it as a regular trouser crotch. The prompt explicitly corrects this.

Crop Top with Lehenga — the midriff gap is the critical detail. Skin tone in the exposed midriff must match the user exactly. GPT often either fills in the gap or creates a skin-colour mismatch.

Draped Dress — the hardest category technically. Added a specific rule about radiating fold lines at gather points, which is what makes a draped dress look real vs flat.

Kurti with Jeans — the denim texture rule is important. GPT tends to render denim as a flat navy/blue fill without the diagonal weave texture that makes it look like actual denim.

Cocktail Dress — the neckline variety is extreme (6 different types) and each has a different failure mode. Strapless dresses float. Off-shoulder fabric curls. Sequin dresses render as flat shiny surfaces. Every one of these is explicitly corrected.

Party Saree — different from the standard saree prompt because the fabric types are completely different. Designer Lehenga — the most complex prompt in the entire library. The four embellishment types (zari, kundan, 3D floral, mirrors/sequins) each have completely different light behavior. Why winter wear prompts are the most technically demanding after party wear:

Puffer Jacket — the single biggest failure in GPT for outerwear. It renders the channels as flat stripes, not inflated tubes. The explicit instruction that each channel must look rounded and three-dimensional with stitch-line valleys is the key fix.

Leather Jacket — GPT renders leather as plastic. The three rules that fix this: sharp specular highlight (not soft), flex creases at the elbow and armpit (always present on leather), and grain texture visible on the surface.

Sweater — cable knit is where GPT fails most. It draws the cables as a printed pattern rather than a three-dimensional rope twist. The instruction that the cable must show the braid effect with shadows between the raised sections is what produces a real result.

Poncho — GPT always tries to fit it like a jacket. The explicit rules that it has no fastening, no sleeves, drapes from the shoulders by gravity, and must show the user's arms emerging from the open sides corrects this default behaviour.

Denim Jacket — the diagonal twill weave instruction is the critical addition. Without it, GPT renders denim as a plain coloured fabric. The weave texture at 45 degrees is what makes it look like actual denim.
